# What Size Oil Tank Do I Need? 100, 200 or 250 Gallons

The driver tops you up, the invoice lands, and by the third week of January you're squinting at the gauge again. ## Why 200 gallons is the Avalon default

A 200-gallon tank is the size you'll find behind most oil-heated houses from Mount Pearl to Bay Roberts. It's big enough that a normal winter doesn't turn into a standing appointment with the fuel truck. It's small enough to fit a side yard, a basement corner or the spot under a roof overhang that the last owner picked in 1987.

Installed, a painted steel 200-gallon tank is $3,200, inside or outside, and that includes taking the old tank away. Every figure we work from sits in the [oil tank replacement cost guide](/oil-tank-replacement-cost/), so you can check the numbers without ringing anyone.

If oil heats the whole house and you aren't sure, 200 gallons is the safe pick. Going smaller to save a few dollars buys you more deliveries, and the delivery you'll resent is the one in the middle of a northeaster.

## When a 100-gallon tank is enough

A 100-gallon tank costs $3,100 installed, which is $100 less than the 200. That's the whole saving, so the smaller tank isn't a bargain. It's just the right size for one kind of house.

Thousands of Avalon homes added ductless heat pumps over the last few years and kept the oil furnace for the coldest weeks. When a heat pump carries most of the load, oil sits in the tank for months. A smaller tank means less old oil and less air space for condensation. Our [heat pump and oil backup tank article](/blog/heat-pump-oil-backup-tank/) covers that decision in full, so we'll leave it there.

If oil still heats every room in January, don't pick the 100 to save $100. You'll be arranging deliveries about twice as often, and the first real cold snap will make that feel like a bad trade.

## What a bigger oil tank does and doesn't get you

Tanks larger than 200 gallons exist. A 250-gallon tank holds about 1,100 litres, and bigger ones are out there. There's no published price for them, so they're quoted case by case, and the figure depends on the tank, the location and the install.

The regulations cover heating oil systems of 2,500 litres or less, which is where any house is likely to land. You can read the [regulation text](https://www.assembly.nl.ca/legislation/sr/regulations/rc030060.htm) if you want the s.3 wording yourself.

A bigger tank gets you fewer deliveries and a longer runway if a storm keeps trucks off the road. It doesn't get you a longer tag. The legal life of a steel tank comes from the s.15 table, not from the volume, so a 250 and a 200 of the same type run out on the same schedule. A big tank that sits half empty all year also has more air space for condensation, and that's how steel tanks rust from the bottom, in the one spot you can't see.

## Space, stairs and deliveries

The tank has to fit where it's going, and that often decides the size before anything else does. Picture a 1980s bungalow in Mount Pearl with a narrow side yard between the house and the fence. A 200-gallon tank sits on its legs with room to spare, but a bigger one might not leave you space to get around it with a shovel in February. ## Size doesn't change the tag date

It's easy to spend an hour on gallons and thirty seconds on the material. It should be the other way round. Under s.15(1), a single-wall mild-steel tank gets somewhere between 10 and 25 years depending on gauge, outlet and whether it's inside or outside. Our [life table article](/blog/how-long-do-oil-tanks-last/) lays that out, and volume doesn't appear in it anywhere.

Fibreglass and double-wall tanks play by different rules. Their life is set by the administrator, capped at 50 years. An installed fibreglass tank is $3,850, which is $650 more than steel. The [fibreglass tank page](/fibreglass-oil-tank-upgrade-st-johns/) explains the upgrade, and [steel vs fibreglass](/blog/steel-vs-fibreglass-oil-tank/) compares how each one ages.

So if you're torn between a 200 steel tank and a fibreglass tank, the material question is the bigger one. Pick the size to fit the house, then pick the material to fit how long you plan to stay.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many gallons is a typical home oil tank?
Most oil-heated Avalon homes run a 200-gallon tank, which holds about 900 litres. Homes where a heat pump does most of the heating often go with 100 gallons, about 450 litres. Larger tanks are available for specific sites.

### Can I put in a smaller tank when I replace mine?
Yes, the size is your choice as long as it suits the house and the space. A new system is registered by the installer's licensed inspector either way, so the tag on the new tank starts fresh. Just don't shrink the tank if oil still heats your whole house.

### Does a bigger oil tank last longer before it expires?
No. Legal life under s.15 depends on the material, the gauge for steel, the outlet position and whether the tank is inside or outside. Gallons don't enter into it.

### Does a bigger tank cost much more?
Between 100 and 200 gallons the gap is $100, with the 100 at $3,100 and the 200 at $3,200. Tanks above 200 gallons have no published price, so they're quoted case by case. Ask for the figure in writing before you decide.
