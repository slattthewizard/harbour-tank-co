# Oil Tank Repair or Replace? What Can Be Fixed on a Leaking Tank in NL

The stick comes up wet at the bottom, there's a dark stain on the gravel, and the furnace is still humming away. Every homeowner in that spot asks the same thing: is this an oil tank repair or replace situation? If the leak is in the tank shell itself, the answer is replace, and it's kinder to hear that from us now than from a delivery driver later.

This guide separates the fixes that are real from the ones that only look like fixes. Plenty of things on an oil system can be repaired, and often are. The tank wall isn't one of them. ## What the regulations say once a tank leaks

The Heating Oil Storage Tank System Regulations (NLR 60/03) are blunt about leaks. An owner who believes oil is leaking, or that water is getting into the system, has to inform the province's environment department straight away (s.18(1)). They also have to tell their heating oil delivery company (s.18(3)).

Once the delivery company knows, it can't deliver again until the system's integrity has been verified, or it has been repaired or replaced to the administrator's satisfaction (s.18(4)). So on top of the mess, you've got a house that gets no more oil until the problem is settled.

Then comes the clock. Normally you get 30 days after disconnecting a tank to empty it, remove the sludge and residual oil, dismantle and dispose of it, deal with the piping and restore the site (s.16(3)). After a leak or water intrusion, s.18(5) squeezes that to 10 days, with another 30 days for remediation. You can read the whole thing on the [regulation page](https://www.assembly.nl.ca/legislation/sr/regulations/rc030060.htm).

Ten days in a St. John's winter is tight. That's why the decision shouldn't wait for the third phone call. What to do in the first hour is covered in our [first-hour leak guide](/blog/oil-tank-leaking-what-to-do/), so this article picks up after that, with the question of what's left to decide.

## Oil tank repair: what can be fixed without replacing the tank

The tank is only one part of the system. The regulations define the system as the tank plus the vent, fill and withdrawal piping and the filters, right up to where the line meets the furnace (s.2(n)). A good share of what looks like a tank leak is really a leak somewhere else on that chain.

These are the usual repair candidates:

- a weeping fitting at the filter or a valve
- a cracked or seized gauge
- a rusted or broken vent cap or vent pipe
- a kinked, crushed or weeping copper line
- rusted legs on a tank that still has years of legal life left

If the leak is at one of those and the tank shell is dry and sound, a repair can be the sensible call. We'd always rather replace a part than a tank, if the tank is fine. Part and labour prices are quoted case by case.

There's a catch. The regulations define "alter" broadly: to repair, replace, add or remove any part of the system counts (s.2(d)). An altered system has to be inspected by a licensed inspector (s.12(3)), and reasonable spill precautions apply while the work is underway (s.12(2)). So even a small fix isn't a handshake job. Ask whoever does the work how the inspection gets handled before they start.

One more thing in your favour. A licensed inspector must recommend only necessary repairs to, or replacement of, a system (s.20(4)(b)). If an inspector says the tank has to go, ask which deficiency they're pointing at. That's a fair question and a good inspector answers it plainly.

## Why patches, epoxy and putty don't fix the problem

Somebody will tell you about a putty stick, an epoxy kit or a sheet-metal patch with sealant. They may even slow a drip for a few days. They don't solve the actual problem, for four reasons.

First, a leak means the wall has failed. Steel tanks rust from the inside out, because condensation leaves water on the tank floor, so the thin spot you can see is rarely the only thin spot. Second, the tag date doesn't move. A patched tank is the same age it was yesterday, and s.15 counts from the effective date on its registration.

Third, the delivery side doesn't care about your patch. Once the delivery company knows about a suspected leak, s.18(4) stops deliveries until the integrity is verified or the system is repaired or replaced to the administrator's satisfaction. A dab of epoxy isn't going to satisfy anyone. Fourth, and this is the part we feel strongest about, we wouldn't put our name to a patched tank. It's a leak waiting for the next freeze-thaw cycle.

Surface rust is a different matter, and our [rust guide](/blog/rust-on-oil-tank/) covers which kind is cosmetic and which means replace. A weeping hole isn't cosmetic. The department also publishes a [PDF on oil tank damage](https://www.gov.nl.ca/eccc/files/env-protection-petroleum-pdf-oil-tank-damage.pdf) that spells out what owners must do.

## Oil tank repair or replace: a quick reference

Here's how the common problems sort out. Where a row says "usually", the final call belongs to the inspector and the contractor looking at the actual tank, not to us from a photo.

| Problem | Repair or replace | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeping fitting at the filter or valve | Repair | The leak is in a part and the shell is dry. It's still an alteration, so an inspector gets involved. |
| Cracked or seized gauge | Repair | The gauge swaps out and the tank stays. |
| Rusted or broken vent cap or pipe | Repair | Part of the system, quick to replace, easy to forget. |
| Crushed, kinked or weeping copper line | Repair, usually | Replace the run, and check for oil smell along it. |
| Pinhole or seep in the tank shell | Replace | The wall has failed, and the s.18 clock applies. |
| Water or heavy sludge keeps coming back | Replace, usually | Often a sign of corrosion inside the tank. |
| Bulging, split seam or cracked fibreglass | Replace | Structural damage can't be patched. |
| Rusted legs on a tank with a long tag | Repair the stand | The tank is sound, so fix the footing. |

If you're not sure whether water is your problem, our [water in oil tank guide](/blog/water-in-oil-tank/) explains how it gets in and how to check.

## When a repair still makes sense on an older tank

Age matters here, and it's the part people skip. Picture a 1980s bungalow in Paradise where the filter fitting weeps, the shell is dry underneath and the tag is stamped 11-2033. The legs are solid. Replacing the fitting and the filter is a sensible repair, because the tank has years of legal life left and the leak isn't in the tank.

Now say your tag reads 3-2027 and there's a seep along the bottom seam. Repairing anything on that tank is money into a tank with months of legal life, before you even count the leak. The tank is coming out either way, so put the money into the new one. If you're in Paradise, our [Paradise service page](/oil-tank-service-paradise/) shows how a local swap runs.

The department's own example is that "7-2028" means the tank may not be used after 31 July 2028. If the tag has already run out, the [expired tank emergency page](/expired-oil-tank-emergency-st-johns/) is the faster read.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I patch a small oil tank leak myself?
A patch might slow a drip, but it doesn't restore the tank wall or satisfy the rules. You still have to report a suspected leak to the department (s.18(1)) and tell your delivery company (s.18(3)), and deliveries stop until the system is verified or replaced. If it's safe, stop the flow and keep people and pets away, then call the province's 24-hour environmental emergency line.

### How fast does a leaking tank have to come out?
After a leak or water intrusion, s.18(5) compresses the usual 30 days to 10 days for emptying, removing sludge and oil, dismantling and disposing of the tank, and dealing with the piping. Another 30 days is allowed for remediation. Ask your installer how they'll hit that timeline before you book.

### Can an inspector tell me to replace a tank that looks fine?
An inspector has to recommend only necessary repairs or replacement (s.20(4)(b)). A tank can look fine and still be past its legal life, since age is judged from the s.15 table and not from appearance. If you disagree, ask which deficiency or date they're relying on.

### Does repairing a part reset my tag date?
No. Swapping a fitting, gauge or line doesn't make the tank younger, because s.15 counts from the effective date on the tank's registration. A new tank gets a new registration and a new expiry, which is one reason replacement beats repair on an old tank.
