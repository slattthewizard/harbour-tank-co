# What's in an Oil Tank Replacement Quote (and What Should Be On It)

Two quotes land on your kitchen table the same week. One says $3,200. The other is a few hundred dollars less, and it's tempting, because the tag on your tank runs out next month and the furnace doesn't care what's in your chequing account.

Here's the thing about an oil tank replacement quote: the number at the bottom only means something once you know what's above it. A quote that skips the old tank's removal, the HOST inspection, or the new oil line isn't cheaper. It's just shorter. This guide walks through every line that belongs on a quote in St. John's and across the Avalon, what a fair price looks like in 2026, and the gaps that catch people after the truck has already left the driveway.

## Why a written oil tank replacement quote matters more in Newfoundland

In most of the country, swapping an oil tank is a maintenance job. Here, it's a legal one. Under the province's [Heating Oil Storage Tank System Regulations (NLR 60/03)](https://www.assembly.nl.ca/legislation/sr/regulations/rc030060.htm), every tank system has to be registered and carries a metal HOST tag stamped with a six-digit number and an expiry in month-year form. Once that date passes, no oil company can legally fill it. The driver is required to report the problem, not just skip your house.

That changes what a quote has to do. It's not only a price. It's a promise that when the work is done, a licensed inspector will tag and register the system so your delivery company can come back. If the quote doesn't say that in writing, you don't have a complete quote yet.

The regulations also put a clock on the old tank. Within 30 days of disconnecting it, the owner has to empty it, clean out the sludge, dismantle it, deal with the piping, and restore the site. After a leak or water getting in, that window shrinks to 10 days. "Owner" means you, not the installer, unless the paperwork says the installer is handling it.

So a quote in this province really has three jobs: the new tank, the old tank, and the paperwork. Miss any one of them and there's a second bill coming.

## The lines every oil tank replacement quote should show

A good quote fits on one page, but it's a busy page. Here's what belongs on it.

**1. The tank, by type, size and gauge.** "200-gallon tank" isn't enough. Painted steel, fibreglass, or a Roth double-wall? If it's steel, 12-gauge or 14-gauge? Bottom outlet or end outlet? Those details decide how long the new tag runs. Under section 15 of the regulations, a 14-gauge outdoor steel tank with an end or top outlet gets 10 years. A 12-gauge bottom-outlet steel tank outdoors gets 20. Fibreglass and double-wall tanks get a service life set by the province, capped at 50 years, and fibreglass tags have been seen stamped out to 2061. If the quote doesn't name the gauge, ask. You're buying years, not just steel.

**2. Removal and disposal of the old tank.** This is the line people miss most. Pumping out what's left, cleaning the sludge, cutting the tank down if it's in a basement, and hauling it to a proper disposal site. It should say "included" or show its own price. Silence isn't the same as included.

**3. Stand, pad or base.** An outdoor tank needs a level, solid footing, usually a concrete pad or patio stones with a steel stand. If your old base has heaved with the frost or cracked, the quote should say whether a new one is in the price or not.

**4. Oil line, filter and fittings.** The line from the tank to the furnace, the filter, the fusible valve, the fittings. Reusing a 20-year-old copper line under a brand new tank is a false economy, and plenty of installers won't do it. The quote should say new or reused.

**5. Vent, fill pipe and whistle.** Every tank needs a vent with an alarm whistle so the driver knows when it's full. It costs little to include and a lot to forget.

**6. What happens to your remaining oil.** If you've got a quarter tank left, is it going into the new tank or into a drum on the truck? Heating oil isn't cheap. A proper quote says where it ends up.

**7. Inspection, tag and registration.** The installer's licensed inspector has to inspect the finished system, attach the HOST tag, and register it with the province. Your delivery company will ask for that tag number before the next fill. This line is the whole reason you're doing the job, so it should be spelled out, not assumed.

**8. Site restoration and cleanup.** Gravel raked back, sod dropped in, basement floor wiped down. Small, but it's the difference between a finished job and a mess you inherit.

**9. Warranty, in years, on both the tank and the labour.** The manufacturer's warranty on the tank is one thing. The installer's warranty on the work is another. Both belong on paper.

**10. Tax.** HST in this province is 15%. A quote should say whether it's in the number or on top. A $3,200 quote before tax and a $3,200 quote after tax are $480 apart.

## Red flags that should send you back to the phone

Look for these.

- **One number, no lines.** "Tank replacement: $X" tells you nothing about what's covered. Ask for it itemized.
- **No mention of the HOST tag, inspection or registration.** Without those, you'll have a new tank and still no oil delivery.
- **Removal missing or buried.** If the old tank isn't on the page, assume it's not in the price.
- **A basement removal priced over the phone.** Nobody can price cutting a tank out of a basement without seeing the stairs, the door and the floor. "Case by case" is the honest answer.
- **No tank type or gauge.** You can't compare a 14-gauge tank with a 10-year tag to a fibreglass tank with a tag out past 2050 on price alone.
- **Pressure to sign today.** Tags expire on a known date. There's rarely a reason to sign on the doorstep.
- **A rebate promise on a new oil tank.** The oil-to-heat-pump programs were for leaving oil, not for new tanks, and the main federal stream closed to new applications at the end of July 2026.
- **No warranty in writing.** A verbal "we stand behind our work" isn't a warranty.

One safety note: don't drain, disconnect or move a tank yourself to save a line on the quote. Heating oil that reaches the ground has to be reported under the province's [Environmental Protection Act](https://www.assembly.nl.ca/legislation/sr/statutes/e14-2.htm), cleanup runs far past the cost of any tank, and the regulations give you 10 days after a leak to have the old tank dealt with. If you smell oil, see a wet patch under the tank, or spot a drip at a fitting, stop and [call the 24/7 line](tel:+17098003117). ## Questions to ask before you sign

Bring this list to the phone or the kitchen table. A good installer will have quick answers.

1. Is the old tank's removal and disposal in this price?
2. Who inspects, tags and registers the new system, and is that included?
3. What gauge and outlet is the steel tank, and how long will the tag run?
4. Is the oil line new or reused?
5. Is HST in this number?
6. What happens to the oil left in my old tank?
7. What's the warranty on the tank, and what's the warranty on your work?
8. If you find something once the old tank's out, how do you handle the change?

That last one matters. Sometimes the base is worse than it looked, or the line is corroded where it goes through the wall. A fair installer tells you before doing the extra work, not after, and puts it on paper. Keep the quote once the job's done, too. The province's own guidance notes that confirmation of registration is often required by an insurance company or by a lawyer or realtor during a sale. A quote that shows the tank type, the install date and the tag number is the easiest paper trail you'll ever have.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should an oil tank replacement quote be in St. John's?

Published retail pricing for Atlantic Canada in 2026 puts a 200-gallon painted steel tank at about $3,200 installed and a fibreglass or Roth double-wall tank at about $3,850, with removal of the old tank included. A quote a little above or below that isn't a warning sign on its own. A quote well below it with no removal, tag or line items usually is.

### Does an oil tank quote include removing the old tank?

It should, and the quote should say so in plain words. Removal and disposal of an outside tank runs about $500 on its own, so a quote that leaves it out can look cheaper than one that includes it. Basement and indoor removals are priced case by case after someone has seen the space.

### Will the quote include the HOST tag and registration?

A complete quote names the inspection, the tag and the provincial registration as part of the job. Without a valid tag, your oil company can't legally deliver, no matter how new the tank is. If the quote is silent on this, ask who the licensed inspector is and whether their visit is in the price.

### Can I get an oil tank replacement quote over the phone?

You can get a ballpark for a standard outdoor swap, and that's a fine place to start. But a firm written quote usually needs a look at the base, the line, the access and the old tank, especially for anything in a basement. Photos help, and a site visit is the only way to price the awkward jobs honestly.
