## The law: NLR 60/03, Heating Oil Storage Tank System Regulations, 2003 (Environmental Protection Act)
- Every registered tank system carries a metal HOST tag with a six-digit number and a stamped expiry in
  MM-YYYY form. Example from the department: "7-2028" means the tank may not be used after 31 July 2028.
- s.15(1) maximum service life of an aboveground mild-steel tank (from effective date, before removal):
  - Outside, single wall: end or top outlet 14 ga = 10 years; 12 ga = 15 years; bottom outlet 14 ga = 15
    years; 12 ga = 20 years.
  - Inside a structure with a foundation: end or top outlet 14 ga = 15 years; 12 ga = 20 years; bottom outlet
    14 ga = 20 years; 12 ga = 25 years.
- s.15(2)-(5): double-wall and non-mild-steel (fibreglass, Roth) tanks get a life set by the administrator,
  capped at 50 years. Fibreglass tags have been seen stamped to 2061.
- s.4(1): operating a system that does not comply with s.15 is prohibited (in force since 1 Aug 2009).
- s.13(1)(b)(ii): heating oil may not be delivered to a system without a valid, unexpired registration label.
  s.13(2): the delivery driver must report deficiencies immediately to the owner and the administrator.
- s.16(3): within 30 days of disconnecting a tank the owner must empty it, remove sludge and residual oil,
  dismantle and dispose of it, remove or cap piping, and restore the site.
- s.18(5): after a leak or water intrusion that sequence compresses to 10 days, plus 30 days for remediation.
- s.20(1): only inspection for registration requires a licence. There is no advertising restriction.
- Confirmation of registration is "often required by an insurance company to insure a home or by a
  lawyer/realtor when selling/purchasing a home" (department wording).
